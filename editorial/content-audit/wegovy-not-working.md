The safest first move is not to stop the injection but to book a review with your prescriber, who can check your dose, your technique and whether anything else is getting in the way. ## Common reasons Wegovy may not appear to be working

Before assuming the medication has failed, it helps to work through the ordinary explanations first. Most stalled progress has a fixable cause rather than a biological one.

You might still be titrating. Wegovy is started at a low dose and increased gradually, and the weight loss most people expect only really shows up once you reach a dose that's actually working for you. If you're still a few steps into the schedule, judging the drug now is judging it too early.

Calorie balance matters more than people expect. Wegovy reduces appetite and slows digestion, but it doesn't erase the effect of regular takeaways, grazing, or the extra calories in alcohol and sugary drinks. The [EMA's product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/wegovy-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf) describes Wegovy explicitly as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, and not a replacement for either.

Adherence is a quieter problem than most people admit to. A missed weekly dose, injecting into the wrong site, or storing the pen incorrectly can all blunt the effect without you noticing anything unusual.

Other common causes include:

- **Dose reductions from side effects**: nausea or stomach upset sometimes push a prescriber to slow titration, which delays results.
- **A genuine plateau**: many people lose weight quickly in the first months, then hit a stretch where the scales barely move even though fat loss may be continuing.
- **Underlying medical factors**: thyroid problems, certain medications and metabolic conditions can all work against appetite suppression.
- **Unrealistic timing expectations**: judging the drug at eight weeks when the real test comes later.

Plateaus in particular can feel like failure when they're often just the body adjusting. It's worth reading about how to [check before panicking about a plateau](https://shiftsometimber.co.uk/articles/weight-loss-plateau-men) rather than assuming the treatment has stopped working altogether.

**Pro Tip:** *Keep a simple weekly log of your dose, injection day, and roughly what you've eaten. It takes two minutes and gives your clinician something concrete to work from at your next review.*

## How Wegovy dosing and timing affect results

Wegovy's dosing schedule is deliberately slow, and that's often the first thing worth checking if results seem disappointing.

The standard titration moves through several steps, typically over about 16 to 20 weeks, before reaching the maintenance dose of 2.4 mg weekly. Increasing gradually reduces side effects, but it also means the dose most associated with meaningful weight loss isn't reached until months into treatment.

![Wegovy titration timeline to maintenance dose](https://media.babylovegrowth.ai/blog-images/organization-55073/1790385218978_Wegovy-titration-timeline-to-maintenance-dose.jpeg)

Trial evidence gives a sense of what to expect once you're there. In the STEP trials, participants combined with lifestyle support lost an average of around 14% to 16% of their body weight by 68 weeks, according to the [STEP 3 trial data](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33625476/). That's a much longer runway than most people assume when they check the scales after a month or two.

A few things affect where you land on that timeline:

- **Starting dose tolerance**: some people move through titration faster or slower depending on side effects.
- **Individual response**: not everyone loses weight at the same rate, even at the same dose.
- **Consistency of lifestyle input**: diet and activity levels alongside the medication shift the outcome considerably.

Clinicians tend to hold off on major decisions until you've been on a stable maintenance dose for a reasonable period, rather than reacting to a slow first month.

## How side effects and tolerability can reduce effectiveness

Side effects are one of the biggest hidden reasons Wegovy underdelivers, mostly because they lead to dose reductions or early stopping rather than because the drug itself has stopped working.

**Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and abdominal pain** are the most frequently reported side effects, according to EMA product information, and they're common enough that prescribers often build slower titration around them.

If these symptoms are severe, some people never get past the early steps of the dosing schedule, which naturally limits how much weight loss follows.

Not all symptoms are equal, though. [MHRA guidance for GLP-1 prescribers](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhra-updates-guidance-for-glp-1-prescribers-and-patients) flags severe abdominal pain as a red flag that needs urgent medical assessment, since it can indicate pancreatitis rather than routine gastrointestinal upset.

Practical steps that often help without needing to stop treatment include eating smaller meals, avoiding very fatty or rich food around injection day, and staying upright for a while after eating. Full guidance on the [side effects timeline and red flags](https://shiftsometimber.co.uk/articles/wegovy-side-effects-timeline) is worth reading if you're managing this stage.

## Medical checks and investigations to consider if weight loss stalls

Sometimes the problem isn't behaviour or dosing at all. A handful of medical conditions and other medications can quietly work against Wegovy's effect, and these are worth ruling out if progress has genuinely stopped.

Conditions and medicines that commonly interfere include an underactive thyroid, corticosteroid use, some antipsychotics, and insulin, all of which can drive weight gain or resist weight loss regardless of appetite suppression.

At a clinical review, it's reasonable to ask for:

- **Thyroid function testing (TSH)** to rule out hypothyroidism as a contributing factor.
- **HbA1c or basic metabolic screening** to check for undiagnosed insulin resistance.
- **A full medication review**, since several common prescriptions can promote weight gain as a side effect.
- **Referral to a specialist weight-management service** if criteria are met and progress remains poor despite the above.

NICE guidance is clear that semaglutide for weight management should sit within [specialist weight-management services](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta875/chapter/1-recommendations), not as a stand-alone prescription, which is part of why these checks and referral pathways exist in the first place.

## Practical steps to improve response while staying safe

Most people can improve their results without changing medication at all. The following steps are worth working through in order:

1. **Tighten up meal structure.** Regular, protein-forward meals reduce the swings in hunger that lead to snacking, and cutting liquid calories such as sugary drinks and alcohol often makes a bigger difference than people expect.
2. **Build activity gradually.** You don't need intense training. Consistent walking, resistance work twice a week, and small daily movement additions support fat loss alongside appetite reduction.
3. **Protect your sleep.** Poor sleep raises hunger hormones and makes cravings harder to manage, undermining the appetite control Wegovy is meant to provide.
4. **Manage stress and alcohol.** Both affect motivation and appetite in ways that quietly cancel out medication benefits.
5. **Use adherence tools.** Weekly reminders, a fixed injection day, and tracking tools make it far less likely a dose gets missed or delayed.

The [NHS's calorie counting guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/lose-weight/calorie-counting/) is a solid, free starting point for structuring meals around a sensible deficit.

**Pro Tip:** *Pick one change from this list to focus on for a fortnight rather than trying to overhaul everything at once. Consistency beats intensity.*

Structured, multidisciplinary support, combining medication with diet, activity and behavioural input, tends to produce better real-world outcomes than medication alone, which is part of why NICE built specialist service delivery into its recommendations.

## Clinical stopping rules, treatment duration and what happens if you stop or switch

Wegovy isn't intended as an indefinite, unmonitored prescription. NICE guidance sets clear checkpoints: treatment is generally reviewed at 6 months on the maintenance dose, and NICE TA875 suggests considering stopping if weight loss is below 5% at that point, with a maximum recommended duration of 2 years within specialist services.

Stopping isn't necessarily the end of the story, though. Evidence from a withdrawal trial found that continuing semaglutide maintained further weight loss, while switching to placebo led to significant regain, with a statistically significant difference between groups over 48 weeks, according to [trial data on maintenance of weight loss](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33755728/). That's a strong signal that whatever weight loss you achieve on Wegovy tends to need an ongoing plan, whether that's continued medication, a structured maintenance approach, or both.

Key points to raise at a review:

- **Ask what your 6-month checkpoint result was** and what it means for continuing.
- **Discuss tapering versus stopping outright** if treatment is ending.
- **Plan for maintenance before you stop**, not after.

## FAQ

### Why am I not responding to Wegovy?

Lack of response is usually down to still being in the titration phase, ongoing calorie surplus, missed doses, or a medical factor such as thyroid dysfunction. A clinical review can check your dose, adherence and any interfering conditions before assuming the medication itself has failed.

### How can I speed up weight loss on Wegovy?

Tightening meal structure, cutting liquid calories, building in regular activity and protecting sleep all support the appetite reduction Wegovy provides. The EMA's product information is explicit that Wegovy works as an adjunct to diet and activity, not instead of them.

### Why am I not losing anything on Wegovy?

This is often a titration or adherence issue rather than a sign the drug has stopped working, since meaningful results in trials generally built up over many months rather than weeks. Missed doses, incorrect injection technique, or an unaddressed medical condition are worth ruling out with your prescriber.

### Why is Wegovy taking so long to work?

Wegovy's dosing schedule increases gradually over roughly 16 to 20 weeks before reaching the maintenance dose, and the largest weight-loss effects in trials appeared well beyond that point, with STEP 3 data showing continued loss out to 68 weeks. Judging the medication too early is one of the most common reasons people think it isn't working.
