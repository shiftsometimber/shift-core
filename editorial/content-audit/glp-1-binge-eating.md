# Binge eating and weight-loss medicines: keep the treatment questions separate

Repeated episodes of eating with a sense of losing control deserve appropriate support. They are not something you have to solve by being stricter, and a weight-loss medicine is not automatically a treatment for an eating disorder.

## Start with an assessment, not a medicine recommendation

Tell a GP or appropriate healthcare professional what happens, how often, how you feel afterwards and how it affects your life. You do not need to prove that your weight is high enough or that the episodes are severe enough to ask for help.

Eating-disorder care needs to consider the whole situation, including mental and physical health. A diagnosis cannot be made from a calorie log, an online quiz or a coaching conversation. If you have already been prescribed a medicine for another purpose, tell the clinician assessing your eating difficulties about it.

## What established guidance says

NICE recommends eating-disorder-focused support for binge eating disorder, starting with guided self-help where appropriate and considering further psychological treatment according to the person's needs. It advises against medication as the sole treatment for binge eating disorder.

The NHS explains that trying to diet during treatment can make it harder to stop binge eating. Discuss food changes and any weight-management treatment with the team looking after you rather than combining conflicting plans yourself.

## Research is not an individual treatment recommendation

Interest in how GLP-1 medicines affect appetite does not establish that any particular medicine is an approved or suitable treatment for your eating disorder. A small or early study is not equivalent to a large controlled trial, a licence or an individual assessment. This article does not recommend starting, stopping or changing a medicine to manage binge eating.

If you are considering a study or a proposed off-label treatment, ask the clinician what evidence supports it, what uncertainty remains, how safety is monitored and what established options are available. Do not buy a medicine without a legitimate prescription or use a product sold as a research peptide.

## Prepare for the conversation

- Describe the episodes in your own words, including the sense of control and the distress involved.
- Mention restriction, fasting, vomiting, laxative use or excessive exercise if these occur.
- Bring your medicine list and tell the clinician about other health conditions.
- Ask how to obtain eating-disorder support locally and what to do while waiting.
- Say if tracking food, weight or calories makes the difficulty worse.

These notes are preparation for care, not a checklist you must complete before receiving help. Seek urgent medical help for an immediate safety concern rather than waiting to build a diary.
