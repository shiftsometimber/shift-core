## Mounjaro cost UK by dose: the official list prices explained

The official list prices for tirzepatide, the active ingredient in Mounjaro, are set out by [NICE guidance TA1026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta1026/chapter/2-information-about-tirzepatide). These are the manufacturer's published prices for a 4-week supply, before any commercial discounting.

| Dose | List price (4 week supply) |
|---|---|
| 2.5mg | £133 |
| 5mg | £180 |
| 7.5mg | £255 |
| 10mg | £255 |
| 12.5mg | £330 |
| 15mg | £330 |

Notice that 7.5mg and 10mg share a price band, as do 12.5mg and 15mg. That's a manufacturer pricing decision, not a clinical one, and it matters when you're weighing up dose escalation costs later in your treatment.

A significant price increase occurs when moving from the 2.5mg starter dose to higher maintenance doses such as 12.5mg or 15mg, reflecting a substantial rise in list price. Anyone budgeting for Mounjaro based on the starter-dose price they saw advertised is likely to be badly wrong within three or four months.

### Why private prices don't match the list price

List price is what NICE and the NHS use as a reference point. It is not what most private patients actually pay. Retail prices from private providers and pharmacies typically sit above or below list price depending on three things: wholesale purchasing contracts, pharmacy margins, and manufacturer rebate arrangements.

Eli Lilly, the manufacturer of Mounjaro, put rebate agreements in place during 2025, and how much of that rebate a pharmacy passes on to patients varies considerably. That's part of why one provider can advertise a 5mg pen at £145 while another charges £189 for what is clinically the same product, according to pricing analysis from [Mounjaro Click Calc](https://mounjaroclickcalculator.co.uk/guides/how-much-does-mounjaro-cost-uk).

There's also a structural shift worth knowing about. Drug Tariff records held by the [NHSBSA](https://dmd-browser.nhsbsa.nhs.uk/vmpp/view/46198) show an effective list-price rise implemented around 1 October 2025. Price rises at the manufacturer level tend to filter through to retail pricing over the following months, so a quote you saw advertised in mid 2025 may no longer reflect what a pharmacy charges today. Always check a provider's current, live pricing rather than relying on a screenshot or a friend's experience from six months ago.

### Why the starter-dose price is a poor guide to real cost

Providers frequently market Mounjaro "from £99" or similar. That figure almost always refers to the lowest strength, 2.5mg, taken for the shortest possible period. It's a genuine price, but it describes month one of treatment, not month twelve.

Independent UK price-tracker snapshots, including data compiled by Monj, indicate that private retail monthly totals vary from the lowest starter prices up to considerably higher amounts for maintenance doses, depending on provider and discounting structure. If you're planning a realistic budget, the number that matters is the maintenance-dose price, not the headline figure that got you through the door.

**Pro Tip:** *When you see a "from £ X" price advertised, ask the provider directly what the same pen costs at 10mg and 15mg. If they're reluctant to give you a straight answer, that tells you something about how the business plans to make its money later.*

## How private provider pricing varies: bundles, discounts and hidden fees

Private Mounjaro pricing in the UK isn't standardised, and providers structure their offers in genuinely different ways. Some charge per pen with a separate consultation fee. Others bundle everything into a single monthly subscription. A few offer steep first-order discounts that quietly disappear once you're locked in as a returning customer.

Bundled subscription models tend to suit maintenance patients best, because they smooth out the cost of consultations, prescriber reviews and delivery into one predictable monthly figure. Pay-as-you-go pricing can look cheaper on paper but often adds consultation and delivery charges back in at each reorder, which stings more once you're on a higher dose and reordering monthly without fail.

Several costs are easy to miss when you're comparing headline prices:

- Consultation or prescriber review fees, if not already bundled into the pen price
- Pen needles, which aren't always included with the injection pen itself
- Sharps bins for safe disposal of used needles
- Delivery charges, particularly for next-day or signed-for shipping
- Repeat-prescription or "reorder" administration fees

None of these individually looks dramatic. Together, they commonly add somewhere in the region of an extra £10 to £40 a month to the advertised pen price, depending on the provider's fee structure. That's the gap between the number in the advert and the number that actually leaves your account.

### First-order discounts vs sustainable pricing

Watch closely for the difference between an introductory discount and a sustainable price. A provider offering 30% off your first month is a perfectly normal way to win new customers, but it tells you nothing about what you'll pay in month four or month fourteen. What matters for budgeting is the returning-customer price, sometimes buried in the terms and conditions or only visible once you've created an account.

Subscription models bring their own trade-off. You typically get a lower per-pen price in exchange for a recurring commitment, and cancelling can involve minimum-term clauses or forfeited introductory pricing. That's not necessarily a bad deal, but read the cancellation terms before you sign up, not after your circumstances change.

There's a second, less obvious risk here too. Some providers stop prescribing once a patient reaches a defined weight-loss threshold or leaves their initial treatment programme, according to flags raised by Monj's analysis of maintenance pharmacies. A cheap introductory provider that isn't built for long-term maintenance can end up costing you more overall, because switching providers mid-treatment often means paying for a fresh consultation and starting the discount clock again.

## NHS vs private: eligibility, cost and realistic timelines

NHS access to Mounjaro does not use private retail pricing, although standard prescription charges can apply in England unless you are exempt. Access is available through specialist weight-management services and is also being phased into primary care for prioritised cohorts, so the route depends on clinical eligibility and local commissioning.

NHS England's [interim commissioning guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/interim-commissioning-guidance-implementation-of-the-nice-technology-appraisal-ta1026-and-the-nice-funding-variation-for-tirzepatide-mounjaro-for-the-management-of-obesity/) implementing NICE TA1026 sets out how tirzepatide is being rolled out through these specialist services, with supply arranged commercially and separately from private retail pricing. Eligibility generally follows a phased approach based on clinical need, meaning not every GP practice or Integrated Care Board is offering referrals at the same pace.

1. **Check your BMI threshold and prior history.** Tier 3 services typically require a BMI above a defined clinical threshold, often alongside weight-related health conditions, and evidence that other weight-management approaches have already been tried.
2. **Ask your GP about local Tier 3 referral pathways.** Availability varies significantly by area and Integrated Care Board, so what's offered in one part of England may not exist yet in another.
3. **Expect local variation.** NHS England is phasing access through specialist services and primary care, and availability depends on your local Integrated Care Board, service capacity and which priority cohort you fall into. Ask your GP or ICB what pathway is operating locally rather than relying on a national waiting-time estimate.
4. **Factor in prescription charges if you do get an NHS prescription.** England charges a standard prescription fee per item, while Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland provide prescriptions free of charge, so where you live in the UK genuinely affects what an NHS prescription costs you.

For many men, the practical issue is whether they meet the current NHS criteria and how quickly their local pathway can assess them. Access varies by area and cohort, so check the current local route rather than assuming either immediate access or a fixed national wait. If you want a fuller picture of how the NHS pathway works in practice, the guide by Shift Some Timber to NHS access in England covers the referral routes in more detail.

## How to compare private quotes so you're not caught out

Comparing Mounjaro quotes properly means looking past the headline number on the landing page. Here's a practical sequence to work through before you commit to any provider.

1. **Confirm the exact dose you'll be prescribed**, not the cheapest dose the provider advertises. Prices vary substantially by strength, and a quote based on 2.5mg tells you nothing about your likely 10mg or 15mg cost.
2. **Ask what's actually included.** Does the price cover the consultation, ongoing monitoring, and delivery, or are these charged separately at each reorder?
3. **Check the repeat-prescription and cancellation terms.** Some subscriptions lock you into a minimum term or claw back a discount if you leave early.
4. **Verify the pharmacy's regulator.** In Great Britain, check the pharmacy on the General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC) register; in Northern Ireland, check the Pharmaceutical Society of Northern Ireland (PSNI) register before handing over payment details.
5. **Convert the pen price to a genuine cost per month at your actual dose**, and compare that figure across providers rather than comparing headline "from" prices against each other.

The maths here is simple but frequently skipped. If a 10mg pen costs £220 and lasts four weeks, that's your monthly figure, full stop, don't compare it against a competitor's 2.5mg starter price of £99 and conclude they're cheaper overall.

**Pro Tip:** *Ask every provider you're considering the same three questions in writing: what will I pay at 10mg, what will I pay at 15mg, and what happens if I want to cancel? Compare the answers side by side rather than the marketing pages.*

Three red flags should make you walk away immediately: pricing that isn't published anywhere and only appears after you've handed over personal details, no mention of ongoing clinical monitoring once you're prescribed, and any pharmacy you can't verify as UK-regulated through the GPhC register.

## What treatment actually costs from starter dose to maintenance

Most tirzepatide treatment plans for weight management run somewhere between 12 and 18 months, with dose escalation happening every four weeks until you reach an effective maintenance dose, typically 10mg or 15mg. That escalation schedule is exactly why total cost estimates matter more than monthly snapshots.

**Here's what three realistic pathways look like in practice.** A low-dose pathway, where someone responds well at 5mg and stays there, might run roughly £180 a month, totalling around £2,160 to £3,240 across 12 to 18 months. A mid-maintenance pathway reaching 10mg, which is common, runs closer to £255 a month using list-price figures, or towards the higher end of the £250 to £340 private-market range reported by Monj's pricing snapshots, totalling roughly £3,060 to £4,590 over the same period. A high-dose pathway reaching 15mg runs around £330 a month at list price, pushing total costs towards £3,960 to £5,940 across 12 to 18 months.

## Insurance coverage and reimbursement for Mounjaro

Private medical insurance in the UK rarely covers weight-loss medication like Mounjaro, even under comprehensive policies. Most UK insurers classify obesity treatment and weight-management medication as an exclusion, in the same category as cosmetic procedures, regardless of how the prescription is justified clinically.

There are limited exceptions. Some employer-provided health schemes include weight-management support as a wellbeing benefit, though this tends to cover consultations or a structured programme rather than the medication cost itself. If you have private health insurance, it's worth checking your policy document specifically for "weight management" or "bariatric" exclusions rather than assuming general prescription cover applies.

Reimbursement through the NHS works differently again. If you qualify for and receive Mounjaro through an NHS Tier 3 pathway, you pay only the standard prescription charge in England, or nothing at all in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. There's no separate reimbursement scheme for patients who pay privately and later want to claim costs back. For most UK residents, the honest answer is that Mounjaro is currently an out-of-pocket private cost or a free NHS prescription, with very little middle ground through insurance.

## Cost-saving tips and patient support in the UK

A handful of practical habits make a genuine difference to what you spend on Mounjaro over a full treatment course. Comparing providers at your actual maintenance dose rather than the advertised starter price, discussed earlier, is the single biggest lever. Beyond that, a few smaller tactics add up.

Buying in larger multi-pen quantities where a provider offers it usually brings the per-pen price down, provided you're confident in your dose stability. Some providers offer loyalty pricing for patients who stay on the same subscription for six months or longer, which rewards exactly the maintenance behaviour that keeps treatment effective. It's also worth asking providers directly whether they offer a price match or review against competitor pricing, since some do this quietly rather than advertising it.

There is no dedicated NHS-run patient assistance scheme that discounts private Mounjaro purchases, and no manufacturer coupon scheme currently operates in the UK equivalent to those seen in other countries. The realistic savings route for most UK patients is comparing genuine maintenance-dose pricing across regulated providers, checking for bundled versus add-on fees, and avoiding the trap of switching providers repeatedly and paying fresh consultation fees each time.

## FAQ

### How much does Mounjaro cost per month in the UK?

Private monthly costs typically range from roughly under £120 at lower doses to £200 to £340 for higher maintenance doses, according to UK price-tracker data. Official NICE list prices run from £133 for 2.5mg to £330 for 12.5mg and 15mg per four-week supply, as set out in NICE guidance TA1026.

### What are the common side effects of Mounjaro?

The most commonly reported side effects are gastrointestinal, including nausea, diarrhoea and reduced appetite, particularly during dose increases. If you experience a suspected adverse reaction, you can report it directly through the [MHRA Yellow Card](https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/) system, the UK's official safety-reporting route.

### Where can I buy weight-loss injections like Mounjaro in the UK?

Mounjaro is available through NHS Tier 3 weight-management services for eligible patients, or through private clinically governed providers. Always confirm any pharmacy dispensing the medication is properly registered before paying.

### What is the injection used for weight loss called?

Mounjaro, containing the active ingredient tirzepatide, is one of the main injectable medications prescribed for weight management in the UK. It works differently from semaglutide-based treatments, and the two are compared in detail in this Mounjaro versus Wegovy breakdown.

### Does private health insurance cover Mounjaro in the UK?

Most UK private health insurers exclude weight-loss medication from standard cover, treating it similarly to cosmetic treatment exclusions. A small number of employer wellbeing schemes support weight-management consultations, but medication costs are almost always paid out of pocket privately.
