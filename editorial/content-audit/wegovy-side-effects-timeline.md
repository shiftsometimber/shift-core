# Wegovy side effects: timing varies, warning signs matter

Side effects do not follow a reliable week-by-week timetable. You may have few symptoms, symptoms after a dose change or problems that need assessment at another point. This guide helps you record what is happening and choose the right contact; it cannot decide whether a symptom is caused by Wegovy.

## What the leaflet tells you

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and constipation are recognised effects of semaglutide. The UK injection leaflet describes gastrointestinal effects as occurring mainly during dose escalation and usually reducing over time. That does not establish a particular worst day, a week when reflux must start or a deadline by which your symptoms should disappear.

The prescribed dose schedule is a treatment plan, not a symptom calendar. Your prescriber decides whether a change is appropriate. Do not increase, reduce, restart or switch a medicine because an online timeline says you ought to have reached a particular stage. Different Wegovy formulations have different instructions.

## What to record for your prescriber

- The exact medicine and formulation, current prescribed dose, last use and any recent change.
- When the symptom began, how often it happens and whether it is improving or worsening.
- Whether you can eat, drink and keep fluids down.
- Any other medicines or supplements and any treatment you have already tried.
- What the problem stops you doing: sleeping, working, eating or ordinary activity.

A short, accurate record is more useful than rating yourself against somebody else's experience. Do not wait to collect a complete diary before seeking help for worrying symptoms.

## Get urgent help for warning signs

**Severe, persistent abdominal pain, especially if it reaches your back:** seek urgent medical attention. The UK leaflet warns that this may be pancreatitis. Follow the leaflet's instruction to stop using Wegovy and contact a doctor immediately for this symptom; do not wait for the next routine review.

**Sudden vision loss or rapidly worsening eyesight:** contact a doctor immediately, following the leaflet. Do not treat it as a normal escalation effect or wait for a scheduled check-in.

**Difficulty breathing, swelling of the tongue or throat, collapse or another serious allergic reaction:** call 999.

**Repeated vomiting, inability to keep fluids down or signs of dehydration:** seek prompt medical advice. A clinician or pharmacist can advise what support is appropriate. This page does not prescribe a laxative, anti-sickness medicine or rehydration treatment for you.

For urgent advice, use the appropriate service where you live: [England](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-use-111/), [Scotland](https://www.nhs24.scot/), [Wales](https://111.wales.nhs.uk/) or [Northern Ireland](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/health-service-useful-numbers). Call 999 for a life-threatening emergency.

## Keep the support roles clear

Ask the prescribing service how to contact it between appointments and what to do if it does not reply. If you cannot reach the service, use an appropriate pharmacy, GP or urgent-care route according to the problem. Do not assume a general support programme is monitoring your symptoms.
