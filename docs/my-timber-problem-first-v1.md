# My Timber — problem-first product contract

## The job

My Timber helps a member decide what to do next when food, symptoms, energy,
treatment or the day itself feels difficult. It remembers what worked and uses
that evidence next time.

It is not a daily questionnaire, habit tracker, calorie counter, generic task
list or another chat box.

## First screen

The member sees useful problems, not health-data fields:

- What should I eat next?
- My stomach feels rough
- I have no energy
- Help me move
- Treatment question
- I am struggling today
- Show my plan

No check-in is required to enter. My Timber asks at most one bounded follow-up
when the answer would materially change the action. Existing member context is
reused silently and shown honestly.

## The return for the member

Every path must produce one immediate asset:

- a named food choice with preparation steps;
- a symptom-adjusted remainder-of-day plan;
- a movement session that can start now;
- governed treatment support or an honest escalation route;
- an emotionally appropriate low-demand plan;
- or the current joined-up day plan.

A path fails acceptance if it ends with only saved data, a generic completion
message or another menu.

## Defensible USPs

1. **Treatment-aware, never treatment-inventing.** Explicit medication context
   can alter appropriate support; missing medicine or dose is never guessed.
2. **One Shift Brain.** Previous choices, active Grub/Fit plans, progress and
   member feedback are reused rather than collected again.
3. **What worked last time.** Accepted, rejected and repeated real-world choices
   change future ranking and explanation.
4. **Whole-day adaptation.** The answer changes with time, symptoms, energy and
   what has already been chosen today.
5. **Bloke-readable evidence.** Advice explains why it fits without clinical
   theatre, diagnosis or unsupported certainty.
6. **Honest low-demand modes.** “Not tonight”, “use what is there” and “I cannot
   face cooking” remain valid inputs, not failures.

## Data bargain

Ask only for information required to improve the current answer. Explain the
effect of the answer in the response. Persist explicit choices and feedback so
the member can see the learning. Do not collect mood, guts or energy merely to
increase engagement metrics.

## Release gate

For each first-screen problem, a new member and a returning member must reach a
useful action in no more than two decisions. The result must remain useful with
no treatment context, preserve account isolation, and never imply monitored
clinical care that does not exist.
