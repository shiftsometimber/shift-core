# SHIFT SOME TIMBER — LTV & Contribution Model

**Date:** 7 September 2026  
**Owner:** Matt O’Brien  
**Status:** WORKING COMMERCIAL MODEL — illustrative, not forecast, not investor numbers

## 1. Rule

Do not put £4k / £10k customer LTV claims into the financial plan until partner costs, conversion rates and retention are observed.

The model exists to answer one question:

> If SHIFT wins a weight-management customer, how much more valuable can that relationship become if stop does not equal churn?

Weight is the acquisition wedge. The value engine is:

**Treatment → Journey → Life Back → Maintenance → MOT → appropriate wider men’s-health care**

## 2. Treatment-only baseline

Illustrative retail assumption only:

- average active treatment revenue: **£210 / month**
- average active period: **12 months**
- treatment revenue LTV: **£2,520**

This is revenue, not profit.

The critical unknown is monthly treatment contribution after:

`medicine acquisition + prescribing + dispensing + fulfilment + delivery + payment fees + refunds / failures`

For working sensitivity only:

| Monthly treatment contribution | 12-month treatment contribution LTV |
|---:|---:|
| £40 | £480 |
| £50 | £600 |
| £60 | £720 |
| £70 | £840 |

Every £5 of contribution per active treatment member is worth:

- 500 active: **£30,000 / year**
- 2,500 active: **£150,000 / year**
- 5,000 active: **£300,000 / year**
- 10,000 active: **£600,000 / year**

This is why the partner tender must compare the whole variable stack, not just pen price.

## 3. Relationship layers after / around treatment

### SHIFT / Journey

Free / acquisition layer. Its economic job is conversion, data continuity and reducing churn; do not force a direct revenue target onto every interaction.

### SHIFT+

Working model only:

- included while actively buying treatment where economics permit
- at treatment stop, offer a lower-cost maintenance relationship
- no hard sell: *Medication may be stopping. SHIFT doesn’t have to.*

Illustrative future pricing sensitivity:

| Monthly SHIFT+ price | 12 months revenue |
|---:|---:|
| £9.99 | £119.88 |
| £12.99 | £155.88 |
| £14.99 | £179.88 |
| £19.99 | £239.88 |

If software / support gross margin later lands around 75–85%, the contribution quality can be materially better than medicine revenue. Do not lock the margin until real service / staffing costs exist.

### SHIFT MOT

Working revenue sensitivity only:

| Retail price | Example partner cost | Gross contribution before overhead |
|---:|---:|---:|
| £99 | £65 | £34 |
| £129 | £75 | £54 |
| £149 | £85 | £64 |
| £179 | £100 | £79 |

The partner-cost column is **not a supplier quote**. Replace it immediately when Thriva / Vitall / DocTap quotes arrive.

### Wider men’s-health services

ED / hair / testosterone investigation / GP care should only enter the model when clinically appropriate and actually live.

Never model every customer as taking every product.

## 4. 1,000-customer cohort sensitivity

Start with 1,000 customers acquired through weight management.

### Treatment baseline

At £210/month × 12 months:

**£2.52m treatment revenue** across the cohort.

At £50 average monthly contribution:

**£600k treatment contribution** across the cohort before CAC and central overhead.

### Post-treatment relationship — illustrative base case

Assumptions:

- 40% convert to SHIFT+ after treatment
- average 12 paid post-treatment months
- £12.99/month price
- 80% contribution margin after direct support/software cost
- 25% purchase one MOT/year
- £54 MOT contribution per purchase
- 10% use one appropriate wider clinical service/year
- £80 average contribution from that service

Result:

| Layer | Cohort revenue / value | Cohort contribution |
|---|---:|---:|
| Treatment | £2,520,000 | £600,000 |
| SHIFT+ post-treatment | £62,352 revenue | ~£49,882 |
| MOT | depends on retail price | £13,500 |
| Wider clinical | depends on retail price | £8,000 |
| **Total contribution** | — | **~£671,382** |

The base illustrative relationship adds roughly **£71 contribution per originally acquired customer** in the first post-treatment year without requiring continued GLP-1 use.

That sounds modest per customer; at scale it matters, and multi-year retention compounds it.

## 5. Three-year contribution LTV sensitivity per acquired customer

These are deliberately broad planning cases — **not forecasts**.

| Layer | Conservative | Base | Strong relationship |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Treatment contribution | £480 | £600 | £840 |
| Post-treatment SHIFT+ contribution | £50 | £150 | £300 |
| MOT contribution | £15 | £45 | £100 |
| Appropriate wider clinical contribution | £20 | £60 | £150 |
| **Illustrative contribution LTV** | **£565** | **£855** | **£1,390** |

The strategic objective is not to manufacture the strong case. It is to make Continuity useful enough that genuine member demand naturally produces it.

## 6. CAC implications

Illustrative only:

| Contribution LTV | CAC £75 | CAC £100 | CAC £150 | CAC £250 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| £565 | 7.5x | 5.7x | 3.8x | 2.3x |
| £855 | 11.4x | 8.6x | 5.7x | 3.4x |
| £1,390 | 18.5x | 13.9x | 9.3x | 5.6x |

These ratios are contribution-LTV / CAC before central overhead, not EBITDA returns.

The point: **we should not spend like a high-LTV business until retention evidence proves we are one.**

## 7. What to measure from day one

Cohort metrics:

- treatment gross contribution per active month
- treatment retention by month / medicine / starting route
- switching rate
- stop rate and reason
- proportion remaining active in Journey at 30 / 90 / 180 days after treatment stop
- SHIFT+ attach while on treatment
- SHIFT+ conversion at stop
- SHIFT+ 3 / 6 / 12-month retention
- Sort intent distribution
- My Next Shift completion rate
- Life Back engagement
- MOT interest rate
- MOT purchase rate once live
- MOT repeat rate
- men’s-health check-in completion
- appropriate ED / hair / other-pathway conversion once live
- CAC by channel
- CAC payback months
- refund / rejection / failed-dispense rate

## 9. Decision thresholds — working, not locked

A useful early commercial target is to engineer the treatment stack toward **£40–£70+ contribution per active member per month** before CAC and central overhead, subject to safe pricing and real partner quotes.

Then prove, rather than assume:

1. stop does not equal total churn
2. members continue using Journey / Life Back
3. a meaningful minority pays for maintenance support
4. MOT demand is real
5. broader men’s-health conversion is clinically appropriate and non-sleazy

## 10. CFO lock

Do not optimise for maximum revenue from one man.

Optimise for:

**useful relationship → retained trust → appropriate next service → better multi-year economics**

Commercial shorthand:

**Win the order → win the stay → earn the next Shift.**
