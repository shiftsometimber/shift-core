## What this pilot is

This is a small, invite-only test of a My Timber feature. It can use information already in a participant's account to suggest a practical change to the current day when plans are disrupted. It does not diagnose, prescribe, decide treatment eligibility or make changes without the participant's explicit confirmation.

The AI model is switched off for this pilot. The live journey uses governed rules and approved Shift Grub and Shift Fit records.

## Information we use

For invited participants, the pilot may use:

- account and membership identifiers;
- My Timber stage, targets and current commitments;
- current check-ins and recent progress history;
- food preferences, dislikes, allergies and dietary flags;
- movement preferences, equipment, location and recorded constraints;
- retained Shift Grub and Shift Fit plan records;
- Life Back priorities and recorded observations; and
- the participant's pilot input, proposal, confirmation choice and resulting Today change.

Some of this is health information and receives additional legal protection as special-category data.

## Why we use it

We use this information only to:

- decide whether the pilot has enough safe, compatible information to operate;
- produce and display a governed same-day rebuild;
- prevent unsuitable, clinical or incomplete proposals;
- write the confirmed change to Today;
- investigate faults and immediate-stop events; and
- answer the agreed commercial pilot questions.

It is not used to train a model, create persistent AI learning, plan multiple days, change Grub or Fit catalogues, make clinical decisions or market unrelated products.

## Proposed legal basis — approval required

The proposed UK GDPR basis for this optional pilot is the participant's consent under Article 6(1)(a). The proposed special-category condition for health information is explicit consent under Article 9(2)(a).

This basis must be confirmed in the signed approval record before invitations are sent. Participation must remain genuinely optional, separate from ordinary membership, specific, informed and as easy to withdraw from as to join.

## Who can see it

Access is limited to the named pilot operators who need it for daily audit, safety, privacy or technical incident handling. Pilot information must not be shared with other members or used by HQ, clinical, prescribing, eligibility, pricing or commercial-gate tools.

The final approval record must identify any service providers that process these records and link to the main privacy information describing international transfers and safeguards, if applicable.

## How long we keep pilot records

Proposed pilot retention: identifiable pilot audit and feedback records are kept for 90 days after the participant's pilot access ends, then securely deleted or irreversibly anonymised.

If an immediate-stop, security, privacy or legal incident occurs, the minimum necessary incident record may be retained under the separately approved incident or legal-retention schedule. The reason and period must be documented. Ordinary My Timber account records continue under the normal service retention policy rather than this pilot period.

The 90-day period is a proposed operational decision and must be approved before use. It must not become indefinite by default.

## Participant choices and rights

Participation is optional. A participant can decline a proposal, leave Today unchanged or withdraw from the pilot at any time by replying through the invitation method or emailing `hello@shiftsometimber.co.uk`.

Withdrawal switches off future pilot access. It does not automatically erase processing that lawfully happened before withdrawal. Participants can also ask about access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and data portability where those rights apply.

Participants may complain to the Information Commissioner's Office. Current contact information is available at `https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/`.

## Automated decisions

The pilot does not make solely automated legal or similarly significant decisions. It proposes a same-day practical change, and nothing is written unless the participant explicitly confirms it.
