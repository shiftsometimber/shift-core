UK Men's Weight & Waist Statistics: 2024 Health Survey

Written and researched by Matt O’Brien, founder of Shift Some Timber.

A citation-friendly summary of official Health Survey for England 2024 statistics on men's weight, BMI, waist size and central adiposity.

If journalists, bloggers, clubs or health organisations need a quick set of current England figures about men's weight and central adiposity, this page is designed to be easy to cite and easy to trace back to the official dataset.

70%of men were overweight or living with obesity in the 2024 Health Survey for England.
86.2kgwas the mean measured weight of men in 2024.
98.4cmwas the mean measured waist circumference among men.
74%of men had a waist-to-height ratio above 0.5, classed as increased or high central adiposity.

What the official data says

The Health Survey for England 2024 reported that 66% of adults were overweight or living with obesity, with the prevalence higher among men at 70% than women at 62%. The same report recorded a mean measured weight of 86.2kg among men.

Central adiposity is also important. Mean male waist circumference was 98.4cm and the mean waist-to-height ratio among men was 0.56. Seventy-four per cent of men had a waist-to-height ratio above 0.5.

Why waist-to-height ratio is included

BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat or describe fat distribution. NHS and NICE guidance therefore use waist-to-height ratio as an additional measure for many adults, with the simple message of trying to keep waist size below half of height where the measure is appropriate.

Citing this page

You are welcome to quote the headline figures above with attribution to the original Health Survey for England 2024. If you also reference Shift Some Timber's summary, please link to this page so readers can follow the source trail.
