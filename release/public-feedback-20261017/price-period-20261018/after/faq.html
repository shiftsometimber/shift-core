FAQs

Proper answers to the questions blokes actually ask.

Written and researched by Matt O’Brien, founder of Shift Some Timber.

Each question opens into a detailed answer with context, practical next steps and clear medical boundaries.

Can men take Mounjaro?

Yes, men can be prescribed tirzepatide (Mounjaro) when it is clinically appropriate and they meet the relevant prescribing criteria.

Read the detailed answer →

Can men take Wegovy?

Yes. Wegovy (semaglutide) can be prescribed to eligible adult men following appropriate clinical assessment.

Read the detailed answer →

Is GLP-1 treatment only for women?

No. GLP-1 and related prescription weight-management medicines are not women's treatments.

Read the detailed answer →

How quickly should a man lose weight?

There is no single perfect weekly rate for every man. A sustainable rate depends on starting weight, health, food intake, activity and treatment.

Read the detailed answer →

Why do men get belly fat?

Men commonly store a greater proportion of fat centrally. Genetics, age, hormones, energy balance, activity, sleep and alcohol can all contribute.

Read the detailed answer →

Does belly fat increase health risk?

Higher levels of central or visceral fat are associated with increased cardiometabolic risk.

Read the detailed answer →

Is BMI accurate for men?

BMI is a useful screening measure, but it does not distinguish muscle from fat and does not directly show where body fat is stored.

Read the detailed answer →

What is a healthy waist-to-height ratio?

NHS guidance uses the simple message of trying to keep your waist below half your height.

Read the detailed answer →

Can walking help weight loss?

Yes. Walking increases activity and energy expenditure and can be a sustainable part of weight management.

Read the detailed answer →

Do I need a gym to lose weight?

No. Walking, home exercise, sport, cycling and ordinary daily movement can all contribute.

Read the detailed answer →

How much protein do men need?

Protein needs vary with body size, age, health, activity and goals. A calculator is only a planning estimate.

Read the detailed answer →

Does sleep affect weight?

Yes. Sleep can influence appetite, food choices, energy, mood and activity.

Read the detailed answer →

Can stress make me gain weight?

Stress can contribute indirectly by affecting eating, alcohol, sleep and activity.

Read the detailed answer →

Does alcohol cause belly fat?

Alcohol provides calories and can influence appetite, food choices and sleep.

Read the detailed answer →

Can I drink alcohol on GLP-1 medication?

There is no one answer for everybody. Alcohol may worsen dehydration or gastrointestinal symptoms for some people, so discuss your situation with your prescriber.

Read the detailed answer →

Can I exercise on GLP-1 treatment?

Many people can remain active, but exercise should match health, fitness and treatment effects.

Read the detailed answer →

Will GLP-1 treatment make me lose muscle?

Weight loss can include some lean tissue. Adequate nutrition and resistance exercise can help support muscle and strength.

Read the detailed answer →

What happens if I stop GLP-1 treatment?

Appetite and weight may change after stopping treatment, so maintenance planning and follow-up matter.

Read the detailed answer →

Is Mounjaro the same as Ozempic?

No. Mounjaro contains tirzepatide; Ozempic contains semaglutide.

Read the detailed answer →

Is Wegovy the same as Ozempic?

They both contain semaglutide, but they are distinct branded products with different licensed uses and dosing pathways.

Read the detailed answer →

Can weight loss lower blood pressure?

For many people carrying excess weight, weight loss can contribute to lower blood pressure alongside other measures.

Read the detailed answer →

Can weight loss improve cholesterol?

Weight management, diet and activity can improve parts of the lipid profile for some people.

Read the detailed answer →

Can losing weight reduce diabetes risk?

For people at increased risk of type 2 diabetes, weight loss and lifestyle change can reduce risk.

Read the detailed answer →

Can weight affect sleep apnoea?

Yes. Excess weight is an important risk factor for obstructive sleep apnoea, although it is not the only cause.

Read the detailed answer →

Why do my knees hurt more after gaining weight?

Greater body mass can increase load through weight-bearing joints, but knee pain can have several causes.

Read the detailed answer →

Does losing weight raise testosterone?

Weight loss may improve testosterone levels in some men living with obesity, but symptoms alone cannot diagnose testosterone deficiency.

Read the detailed answer →

Should men count calories?

Calorie tracking can be useful for some men, but it is not compulsory.

Read the detailed answer →

Are carbs bad for weight loss?

No. Carbohydrate is not automatically fattening. Overall energy intake, food quality and portions matter.

Read the detailed answer →

Is fat bad for weight loss?

No. Dietary fat is essential but energy dense, so portions and the wider diet matter.

Read the detailed answer →

Do I need breakfast to lose weight?

No. The best eating pattern is one that supports good nutrition and can be maintained.

Read the detailed answer →

Can I eat takeaways and still lose weight?

Yes. Takeaways can fit into a weight-management plan depending on frequency, portions, sides and drinks.

Read the detailed answer →

Can I have a BBQ while losing weight?

Absolutely. A barbecue can fit into a healthy week.

Read the detailed answer →

How often should I weigh myself?

There is no single right frequency. The useful part is looking at the trend rather than reacting to one reading.

Read the detailed answer →

Why does my weight change day to day?

Water, salt, carbohydrate intake, bowel contents, exercise, alcohol and timing can all move scale weight.

Read the detailed answer →

What is a weight-loss plateau?

A plateau is a period when body weight changes very little despite continued efforts.

Read the detailed answer →

Is 20% weight loss realistic for everyone?

No. A 20% reduction is a major milestone, not a universal target.

Read the detailed answer →

What is the 20% Club?

A milestone some people use to describe losing 20% of their starting body weight. It is not a universal target.

Read the detailed answer →

Someone who joins Shift and wants useful updates.

No. It is an informational self-check and does not diagnose a condition or establish treatment eligibility.

No. Shift Some Timber does not prescribe or supply medication.
