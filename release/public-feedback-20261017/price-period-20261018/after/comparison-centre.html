How to read a cross-category result

Shared dimensionWhat it meansWhat it does NOT mean
Weight-loss potentialRelative treatment intensity / typical evidenceYour personal predicted loss
SpeedHow quickly meaningful effect/access usually occursA guaranteed timeframe
Lower medical burdenLess procedural/prescription intensity“Safer” for every person
ReversibilityHow easy it is to stop/change/undo the routeThat all biological effects disappear instantly
Lower cost/access burdenRelative financial/commissioning frictionA live provider quote
Maintenance simplicityRelative long-term follow-up/behavioural burdenThat maintenance becomes effortless
EvidenceMaturity/depth of evidence for the option/categoryClinical suitability
ConvenienceDay-to-day/pathway practicalityThe medically best option
The point is not to crown a winner.

Different procedures and medicines have different evidence and practical trade-offs. Neither a numerical rating nor an overall score can establish your clinical suitability. The engine therefore does not produce a single universal winner score.

Universal decision flow

1. Understand the category

Medication, surgery, lifestyle, access route or treatment location.

2. Compare the shared trade-offs

Weight loss, speed, burden, cost/access, maintenance and evidence.

3. Read the category-specific detail

Reflux, injections, nutrition, NHS commissioning, aftercare and other issues that only apply to some options.

4. Overlay your priorities

Use Treatment Finder preferences rather than assuming everyone values the same things.

5. Close information gaps

Use Decision Readiness before turning comparison into a choice.

6. Discuss clinical suitability

The final treatment decision belongs in appropriate shared clinical decision-making.
