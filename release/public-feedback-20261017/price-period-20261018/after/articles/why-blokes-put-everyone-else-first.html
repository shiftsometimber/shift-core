Why Blokes Put Everyone Else First

Looking after other people can fill every spare corner of the week. Your own needs still count, even when there is no neat gap waiting for them.

Identify the part you keep postponing

It might be a health appointment, eating lunch, some sleep or seeing somebody you enjoy. Choose one. A plan to start looking after yourself in general is hard to act on; booking one appointment or arranging one break is more concrete.

Make the workload visible

Write down the commitments that regularly consume the time you expected to have. Discuss one change with the people involved. Could a task be shared, a meeting moved or a regular break protected? If you are a carer, ask your local services about support available to you rather than assuming you must manage alone.

Ask for a practical arrangement

  • ‘I need to attend this appointment. Can we work out cover for that hour?’
  • ‘I'm finding the evenings difficult. Can we split this task differently?’
  • ‘I'd like a short break on Saturday. Which time can we make work?’
  • If the answer is no, ask what alternative is possible instead of abandoning the need altogether.

Do not turn self-care into another performance

You do not need a perfect morning routine on top of an already overloaded day. A sandwich, a brief rest or making the call you have avoided may be enough for the first step. If exhaustion, mood or symptoms persist, seek appropriate advice. A small routine cannot resolve an unsafe workload or replace healthcare; getting support can be part of the plan.
