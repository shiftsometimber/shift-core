Exercise for Weight Loss: Walking, Strength & Mobility – The Practical UK Guide

Written and researched by Matt O’Brien, founder of Shift Some Timber.

A practical UK guide to exercise for weight management: walking, strength training, mobility, step goals, exercise with obesity, joint pain, GLP-1 muscle preservation and a progressive 12-week plan.

Start here: movement is medicine, not punishment

Exercise helps health even when the scale barely moves. It improves fitness, blood pressure, insulin sensitivity, mood, sleep, mobility and muscle preservation. Weight loss itself still depends heavily on overall energy balance, but activity makes the whole system work better.

The goal is not to suddenly become “a gym person”. The goal is to make your body more capable than it was last month.

What do UK guidelines actually recommend?

For adults aged 19–64, NHS and UK Chief Medical Officer guidance recommends:

  • At least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, or
  • 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity per week, or an equivalent combination, and
  • Strengthening activities working the major muscle groups on at least 2 days per week.

Guidance also says to reduce prolonged sitting and break up long periods of inactivity.

Does exercise actually help with weight loss?

Yes — but not in the cartoon version where one workout “burns off” dinner.

Exercise increases energy expenditure and can support weight control. More importantly, it improves fitness and helps preserve muscle while dieting. The NHS explicitly includes increased physical activity as part of losing and maintaining weight.

Do not rely on calorie-burn numbers from watches as permission to eat them back.

Wearables can be useful for trends, but the calorie estimate is not precise enough to run your diet like a bank account.

Why walking is underrated

The NHS calls walking simple, free and one of the easiest ways to get more active, lose weight and become healthier.

A brisk 10-minute walk counts toward the weekly activity target. That matters because it means you do not need an hour-long workout to make progress.

Low barrier

No gym, no kit, no booking.

Scalable

Speed, distance and hills can increase gradually.

Joint-friendly

Usually easier to tolerate than running, particularly at higher body weights.

How many steps should I do?

There is no magic NHS rule saying everybody needs 10,000 steps. The useful target is more than your current baseline.

If you average 3,000 steps, jumping straight to 10,000 can be miserable. Aim for roughly 500–1,000 extra average daily steps, let your body adapt, then progress again.

What counts as brisk walking?

Moderate intensity means your breathing and heart rate increase but you can still talk. The NHS describes brisk walking as a pace where you can talk but would not comfortably sing.

If you can stroll while barely noticing the effort, it is still useful movement — it just may not count as moderate-intensity exercise.

A simple beginner walking plan

Weeks 1–210 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days/week.
Weeks 3–415 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days/week.
Weeks 5–620 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days/week.
Weeks 7–825 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days/week.

This suggested progression is flexible: start lower or repeat a week if needed. It takes you from 50 to 125 brisk minutes per week without a heroic Monday followed by sore knees and three weeks off.

Why strength training matters when losing weight

Weight loss can include lean tissue as well as fat. Strength training gives the body a reason to keep muscle.

Strength also improves everyday function: stairs, getting off the floor, carrying shopping, playing with children, lifting at work and reducing frailty risk later in life.

Why two strength days is enough to start

UK guidance recommends strengthening major muscle groups on at least 2 days each week. That is enough to make meaningful progress for a beginner.

You do not need six gym days. Two full-body sessions done consistently beat an ambitious exercise programme you abandon after 10 days.

Start with 1–2 sets of 8–12 controlled repetitions. Add repetitions first, then resistance.

What should a beginner do in the gym?

A very simple full-body machine session can be enough:

  • Leg press
  • Chest press
  • Seated row
  • Lat pulldown
  • Leg curl
  • Shoulder press if comfortable

Do not worry about looking inexperienced. Everyone in the gym was inexperienced once; most people are too busy checking themselves in the mirror anyway.

Protecting muscle during weight loss

The best combination is:

  • A sensible calorie deficit
  • Adequate protein
  • Strength training
  • Enough sleep and recovery

This becomes more important as weight loss becomes faster.

Exercise while taking Mounjaro, Wegovy or another GLP-1

GLP-1 medicines can reduce appetite sharply, making calorie and protein intake fall quickly. That is one reason strength training matters.

The updated UK Chief Medical Officer physical activity guidance specifically notes the strengthened importance of maintaining strength and balance, including for people using GLP-1 agonist medicines for weight management or diabetes.

  • Do not train hard if you are dehydrated from vomiting or diarrhoea.
  • Prioritise protein and fluids.
  • Use strength work to help preserve lean tissue.
  • Build activity gradually during dose escalation if side effects are significant.

What does mobility actually mean?

Mobility is your ability to move a joint through a useful range of motion with control. It is not the same thing as being able to do the splits.

For weight management, useful mobility means being able to walk, squat to a chair, get on and off the floor, reach overhead and move without avoidable stiffness.

What if my knees, hips or back hurt?

Joint pain is common at higher body weights, but complete inactivity usually makes capacity worse.

Lower-impact options include walking on flat surfaces, cycling, swimming, water-based exercise, seated strength work and gradually increasing resistance.

Pain that is severe, sudden, associated with swelling, giving way, neurological symptoms or follows an injury deserves proper assessment.

Chair-based exercise still counts

The NHS publishes gentle chair-based strength exercises for people who have not exercised much or have mobility limitations.

Examples include sit-to-stands, seated leg work, calf raises and upper-body resistance work. Starting seated is not cheating; it is progression.

Why balance matters

Balance becomes increasingly important with age, deconditioning and rapid weight loss. Simple balance work can include supported single-leg stands, heel-to-toe walking and controlled step-ups.

How do I know when to progress?

Progress one thing at a time:

  • Walk a little longer
  • Walk a little faster
  • Add a hill
  • Add repetitions
  • Add resistance
  • Improve technique

If every week has to be harder than the last, eventually you will hit a wall. Progress over months, not every Tuesday.

What if exercise stops feeling effective?

Your body gets efficient at repeated activity. That is good for fitness but means the same walk may feel easier over time.

Progress by adding pace, hills, duration or strength work rather than trying to double everything at once.

Rest is part of training

Muscle adapts between sessions, not only during them. Beginners generally do well with at least a day between demanding strength sessions for the same muscles.

Walking and light mobility can still be done on recovery days.

How do I tell normal discomfort from injury?

Breathing hard, muscle fatigue and mild delayed soreness can be normal. Sharp pain, joint instability, chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting or new neurological symptoms are not “push through it” signs.

If you have significant cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled blood pressure or serious mobility limitations, ask a clinician about safe exercise progression.

What if I genuinely have no time?

Use shorter blocks. Three 10-minute brisk walks add up to 30 minutes of activity.

A short strength session can fit into a busy day. The NHS specifically notes that activity can be spread across the week rather than done in long workouts.

What if I hate gyms?

You do not need one. Walking, resistance bands, adjustable dumbbells and a chair can cover most beginner needs.

If you do want the gym, book an induction, use a simple machine routine and leave. You do not need to learn gym culture before improving your health.

The 12-week movement plan

Weeks 1–3Build a walking baseline + 1 easy strength session.
Weeks 4–6Move toward 100–120 brisk minutes + 2 strength sessions.
Weeks 7–9Aim near 150 moderate minutes + progress resistance.
Weeks 10–12Maintain 150+ minutes, 2 strength days and add mobility/balance.

The target is not to finish exhausted. The target is to finish with a routine you can still do in six months.

Questions to ask yourself before changing the plan

  • Am I actually moving more than three months ago?
  • Can I walk further before getting tired?
  • Am I stronger?
  • Is joint pain better, worse or unchanged?
  • Am I recovering between sessions?
  • Am I trying to progress too fast?
  • Am I counting one hard workout as permission to sit all week?
  • Do I need lower-impact exercise rather than no exercise?
  • Have I added strength or only cardio?
  • Can I sustain this routine?

Shift Says

If you were our brother...

We'd tell you to walk first, get stronger second and worry about fancy workouts last.

Build toward 150 minutes of moderate movement and two strength sessions a week. If you're nowhere near that today, good — you have somewhere obvious to start.

You do not need to love exercise. You need to make movement normal enough that your body stops treating a flight of stairs like a major sporting event.

Printable movement checklist

  • I know my current activity baseline.
  • I have a realistic weekly walking target.
  • I know what brisk walking feels like.
  • I have at least 2 strength opportunities each week.
  • I include major muscle groups.
  • I use low-impact options if joints need them.
  • I am progressing gradually.
  • I know the difference between training discomfort and warning pain.
  • If using GLP-1 medication, I prioritise protein, hydration and strength.

Exercise FAQs

How much exercise should adults do?

UK guidance recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity per week, plus strength work on at least 2 days.

Does walking count as exercise?

Yes. Brisk walking counts toward moderate-intensity activity.

Is 10 minutes of walking worth it?

Yes. The NHS says a brisk 10-minute daily walk has health benefits and counts toward weekly activity.

Do I need 10,000 steps?

No. There is no universal NHS 10,000-step requirement; increasing from your own baseline is more useful.

How often should I strength train?

At least 2 days per week is the UK guideline for major muscle groups.

Can I exercise with obesity?

Yes. Choose appropriate intensity and lower-impact options where needed.

What if my knees hurt?

Walking on flatter surfaces, cycling, swimming, chair-based exercise and gradual strengthening may help; severe or worsening pain needs assessment.

Should I run to lose weight?

Not necessarily. Walking can be effective and is often more sustainable, especially at higher body weights.

Can strength training slow weight loss on the scale?

It can increase or preserve lean mass, so body composition and waist may improve even when scale change slows.

Does lifting weights burn fat?

It supports energy expenditure and muscle preservation; fat loss still depends on overall energy balance.

Should I exercise every day?

You can be active daily, but hard sessions do not need to be daily.

Can I strength train at home?

Yes. Bodyweight, bands and dumbbells can be enough for beginners.

What exercise is best on Mounjaro?

Walking plus progressive strength training is a practical combination, especially to help preserve muscle.

Can I exercise if I feel sick on GLP-1 medication?

Light activity may be fine, but hard exercise is unwise if you are dehydrated or vomiting.

How fast should I increase activity?

Gradually. Add small amounts of time, steps or resistance and let your body adapt.

Does housework count?

Some housework can contribute to activity, depending on intensity.

Do short exercise sessions count?

Yes. Activity can be accumulated across the week.

What is moderate intensity?

You are breathing faster but can still talk.

What is vigorous intensity?

You are breathing hard and can only say a few words without pausing.

What is the best exercise for weight loss?

The one you can repeat consistently while combining aerobic movement and strength.
