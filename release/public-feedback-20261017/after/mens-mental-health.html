Need urgent help?

If you or someone else is in danger, call 999 or go to A&E. In England, for urgent mental-health help without immediate danger, use NHS 111 online or call 111 and select the mental-health option. Elsewhere in the UK, use the urgent support guide for your nation.

See current NHS urgent mental-health support →

Need someone to talk to? Call Samaritans free on 116 123, 24 hours a day. CALM’s suicide-prevention helpline is free on 0800 58 58 58, 5pm–midnight every day.

Life can get bloody hard. Work, money, relationships, kids, health, weight, grief, loneliness — sometimes several of them at once. You do not need a diagnosis to admit you're struggling, and you do not need to wait until everything falls apart before talking.

How are you feeling?

Low or flatWhen you're not necessarily crying — you just don't feel like yourself. Anxious or overthinkingWorry, dread, racing thoughts and never quite switching off. Stressed or overwhelmedWhen everything feels like another thing on the pile. Angry or irritableWhen struggling comes out as snapping, frustration or a short fuse. Numb or disconnectedNot particularly sad. Not particularly anything. LonelyYou can have a family, mates and colleagues and still feel alone. Low on confidenceSelf-worth, comparison, shame and feeling you've lost yourself. Not sleepingWhy sleep and mental wellbeing can become a vicious circle.

You don't have to know what to call it

Mental-health problems do not always arrive with a neat label. NHS guidance lists feelings such as stress, anxiety, low mood, loneliness, grief and anger among common experiences people may need help with. If the way you're feeling is affecting everyday life, relationships, work, sleep or your ability to cope, that is reason enough to take it seriously.
