GHIC/EHIC is not your complication insurance for planned surgery abroad.

UK government guidance states that GHIC/EHIC does not cover travelling abroad for elective surgery or other planned treatment. It is also not an alternative to travel insurance and does not cover private healthcare or repatriation.

BOMSS warning:

The British Obesity & Metabolic Surgery Society advises UK residents to be cautious about bariatric surgery abroad. Key concerns include inadequate pre-operative assessment, variations in procedure, short stays, poor or absent routine postoperative follow-up, nutritional deficiency and loss of direct access to the original bariatric team if late complications develop.

Why two years of follow-up matters

BOMSS guidance describes regular specialist postoperative follow-up for a **minimum of two years**, followed by at least annual nutritional monitoring and appropriate supplementation in long-term shared care. That is why “WhatsApp us if you need anything” is not the same thing as bariatric aftercare.

What should be in your records before you fly home?

Operation details

Exact procedure, operative note, anatomy created, device/staple information where relevant, surgeon and hospital details.

Clinical course

Discharge summary, complications, medicines, thrombosis prevention, allergies and relevant investigation results.

Follow-up plan

Diet stages, protein/fluid guidance, supplement prescription, blood-test schedule, red flags and named escalation contacts.

Red flags before paying a deposit

Salesperson is your main clinical contact

You should know and be able to verify the operating surgeon and medical team.

Same-day “approval” from BMI + photos

Bariatric suitability needs a proper medical assessment, not merely a sales qualification.

Pressure discount

A countdown timer or “book today” saving is a terrible reason to choose irreversible surgery.

No written complication policy

If nobody can tell you who treats/pays for a leak or readmission, the cheap quote is incomplete.

Aftercare ends at the airport

Routine specialist bariatric follow-up is part of the treatment.

“The NHS will do your bloods”

Do not assume an NHS GP or bariatric service will provide the specialist follow-up omitted from a private overseas package.
