Mounjaro, injectable Wegovy, the new oral Wegovy formulation, liraglutide and Orlistat compared across efficacy evidence, convenience, NHS context, maturity, side-effect burden and maintenance implications.

2026 matters here.

Wegovy is no longer just the familiar 2.4 mg weekly injection story. UK product information now includes a 7.2 mg weekly injectable maintenance option for adults with obesity if needed, and a once-daily 25 mg tablet formulation. Comparisons that ignore those changes are already dated.

What the evidence actually tells us

Mounjaro vs injectable Wegovy

SURMOUNT-5 provides a genuine head-to-head anchor: tirzepatide produced greater mean weight loss than semaglutide 1.7/2.4 mg at 72 weeks. That trial does not answer tirzepatide versus the newly licensed 7.2 mg Wegovy dose.

Oral Wegovy

OASIS 4 gives direct phase 3b evidence for oral semaglutide 25 mg: mean weight change was -13.6% at 64 weeks under the treatment-policy analysis.

Liraglutide

Saxenda has a mature obesity evidence base, but average loss is lower than the newer weekly incretin options and it requires daily injections.

Where Ozempic fits

Ozempic contains semaglutide, but it is not the UK weight-management brand/licence. Wegovy is the semaglutide product licensed for obesity/overweight weight management. We include Ozempic only because confusing the two is one of the most common medication questions.

What we deliberately do not do

  • We do not convert trial averages into a promise for an individual.
  • We do not treat a 10/10 rating as a clinical recommendation.
  • We do not pretend NICE recommendation equals immediate NHS availability.
  • We do not publish a fixed private price as though it will still be correct next month.
  • We do not rank Ozempic as a licensed UK obesity-treatment alternative to Wegovy.
