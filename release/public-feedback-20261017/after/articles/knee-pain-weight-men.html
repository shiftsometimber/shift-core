MEN OVER 40 · KNEES

Knee pain and weight: the answer is not simply “move more”

Extra body mass can increase load through weight-bearing joints, but knee pain has many causes. Telling somebody to run through it is not a plan. The useful approach is to understand the problem, reduce aggravating load where possible and rebuild movement progressively.

Written and researched by Matt O’Brien. Evidence checked 2026-08-26.

Quick takeaways

  • Weight can affect knee load, but it is not the only cause of pain.
  • Pain is not proof of laziness or damage.
  • Lower-impact movement and strength may be useful.
  • Swelling, locking, instability or significant injury need assessment.

Weight and knees

Carrying more weight can increase mechanical demand on the knees and is associated with osteoarthritis risk and symptoms. Losing some weight may improve pain and function for some people, but pain should not be reduced to a blame story.

Previous injury, arthritis, tendon problems, strength, movement patterns and other conditions can all matter.

Keep moving, but choose the right dose

Walking, cycling, swimming, controlled gym machines and progressive strength can provide options when running or football currently flares symptoms. Start below the level that causes a prolonged reaction and build gradually.

A physiotherapist or suitable clinician can help when you are unsure what the knee will tolerate.

Strength is part of the route

Building the muscles around the hip and knee can support function and confidence. Technique and progression matter more than trying to prove toughness on day one.

If a session causes sharply increasing pain, returning swelling or loss of movement, stop and reassess rather than pushing through for pride.

When to seek help

Seek appropriate assessment after significant injury or where pain is severe, worsening, associated with marked swelling, locking, giving way, redness, fever or inability to bear weight.

This page cannot diagnose the cause of knee pain.

Frequently asked questions

Will losing weight cure knee pain?

Not necessarily. It may reduce load and improve symptoms for some people, but knee pain can have several causes.

Should I stop exercising if my knees hurt?

Not automatically. Activity may need modifying, and persistent or concerning pain should be assessed.
