The rules behind the service.

Our privacy information should describe what the service actually does. A new feature or partner does not inherit permission to use every piece of member information.

The current My Timber scope

SHIFT runs account and member tools. Optional health tracking supports the progress, check-in and personalisation features a member chooses. The everyday coach provides non-clinical support; it does not prescribe or diagnose.

InformationPurpose and control
Account and contact detailsAccess, settings, security and support; account controls and rights requests.
Measurements and check-insChosen progress and personalisation features; explicit health consent, export and supported history erasure.
Progress photographsThe progress feature requested; the relevant separate photograph choice and deletion controls.

Security, consent and privacy recordsProtection and accountability; restricted access and purpose-specific retention.

Before a healthcare or testing hand-off

We must identify the organisations involved, their responsibilities, the minimum information each needs and the purpose of each exchange. That includes what comes back to SHIFT, not just what we send.

The arrangement must address the legal grounds for health information, appropriate contracts, staff access, security, international transfers, retention, complaints and data rights. Giving a partner permission to view a questionnaire is a disclosure.

A clinician or laboratory is not automatically a processor. Independent clinical decisions and record-keeping can carry independent controller responsibilities. Where organisations genuinely determine processing jointly, the joint responsibilities must be made clear.

Assessing change

The existing non-clinical tracking assessment is not a blanket assessment of prescribing, blood testing or every AI feature. We must reassess material changes before new processing starts, and explain new purposes to the member. Consent, where required, must be specific, recorded and withdrawable.

Access, retention and accountability

Access must follow the task and the person’s role. A partner should not receive the whole member account where selected information is sufficient. Retention must distinguish optional history, necessary business records and a provider’s medical record. A deletion request must not be described as completed until its outcome is known.
