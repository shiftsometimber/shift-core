GLP-1 NHS Eligibility: Who Qualifies for Weight Loss Injections

Published 3 November 2026 · SHIFT Team

In England, NHS access to weight-loss injections depends on the medicine, the reason for treatment and the available service. England's current primary-care tirzepatide (Mounjaro) rollout generally requires a BMI of at least 35, adjusted for specified ethnic backgrounds, and at least four of five listed weight-related conditions. Specialist-service routes have different criteria. Semaglutide for weight management under NICE TA875 is provided through specialist services; a separate cardiovascular-risk indication has different rules. This guide explains those distinctions and what to ask next.

TL;DR

  • England's current primary-care tirzepatide (Mounjaro) rollout generally requires a BMI of at least 35 and at least four of five listed conditions. The broader NICE recommendation and specialist-service routes differ.
  • The BMI threshold is lowered by 2.5 for people from certain ethnic backgrounds.
  • Access is phased by cohort over three years, starting with those in the highest clinical need.
  • For NHS weight management under NICE TA875, semaglutide is provided through a specialist weight management service. Other treatment indications have separate guidance.
  • Not eligible yet? There is still NHS and other support that does not depend on a prescription.

The short answer: who is eligible on the NHS?

The NHS is rolling out weight management injections in phases. NHS England says that, in the primary care rollout, access initially goes to people with the highest clinical need: adults with a BMI of 35 or more (adjusted for ethnicity) and four listed weight-related health problems.

The detail for tirzepatide, as set out by NHS Cheshire and Merseyside, is a BMI of at least 35 (32.5 for people from minority ethnic family backgrounds) and at least four of these five conditions:

  • type 2 diabetes
  • high blood pressure
  • abnormal blood fats (such as high cholesterol)
  • obstructive sleep apnoea
  • established cardiovascular disease

These rules apply in England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own health services and may differ, so check with your local health board.

How the phased rollout works

The NHS cannot treat everyone who might benefit at once. Mounjaro access is therefore opened up in cohorts over the first three years, according to NHS Cheshire and Merseyside:

CohortWho it coversWhen
Cohort 1BMI of 40 or more with at least 4 listed conditionsFrom the start of the rollout in 2025
Cohort 2BMI of 35 to 39.9 with at least 4 listed conditionsFrom 23 June 2026
Cohort 3BMI of 40 or more with at least 3 listed conditionsFrom 1 April 2027

In practice, a man with a BMI of 37 and four of the conditions may be within the current primary-care cohort. A man with a BMI of 42 and three conditions does not meet that cohort, but can ask whether a specialist-service route or other support is appropriate. A planned future cohort is not a promise of a prescription or a confirmed local start date. Your GP practice or integrated care board (ICB) can explain the arrangements in your area.

BMI thresholds and ethnicity adjustments

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. The NHS defines a BMI of 25 to 29.9 as overweight and 30 or more as obese, but the thresholds for these injections are higher, as above.

For tirzepatide, semaglutide and liraglutide, NICE says to use lower BMI thresholds, usually by 2.5, for people from the listed ethnic backgrounds. The reason, according to NICE, is that some groups face the same obesity-related health risks at a lower BMI. So the adjusted figure of 32.5 is about fairness of access, not a loophole.

If you think the adjustment applies to you, ask your GP to confirm. They will check which backgrounds are listed.

Tirzepatide vs semaglutide vs liraglutide

The route to treatment depends on the medicine.

Tirzepatide (Mounjaro). NHS England says it may be prescribed in primary care for eligible patients, or through a specialist service if a healthcare professional thinks it is right for you.

Semaglutide (Wegovy). For NHS weight management under NICE TA875, this is prescribed through a specialist weight management service. NICE recommends it alongside diet and activity for adults with at least one weight-related condition and a BMI of at least 35, or a BMI of 30 to 34.9 if they meet the referral criteria for specialist services. Your GP would normally refer you to what is called a tier 3 service.

Semaglutide also has a separate NHS indication. NICE TA1152, published on 7 May 2026, covers cardiovascular-risk reduction for adults with established cardiovascular disease and a BMI of at least 27. That guidance does not specify a treatment setting. Its criteria and treatment duration must not be inferred from the weight-management appraisal. Ask your clinician which recommendation applies to your care.

Liraglutide (Saxenda). NICE recommends it for adults with a BMI of 35 or more who have non-diabetic hyperglycaemia and a high cardiovascular risk, and it is prescribed in secondary care. If you are weighing up the two older options, our Wegovy vs Saxenda comparison sets them side by side.

Meeting the criteria does not guarantee a prescription. A clinician still has to assess whether the medicine is right for you.

What happens once you qualify

Expect more than a prescription. NHS Cheshire and Merseyside explains that NHS tirzepatide treatment involves a clinical assessment and taking part in wrap-around care covering diet, nutrition and physical activity. NICE says these medicines should be used alongside a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity.

Then comes review:

  • Regular monitoring. Your healthcare team checks your progress and whether treatment is still appropriate.
  • A six-month checkpoint. For tirzepatide, NICE TA1026 calls for a decision about continuing treatment if less than 5% of starting weight has been lost after 6 months on the highest tolerated dose, taking the person's benefits and risks into account. Do not stop or change treatment yourself; discuss it with your prescriber. For semaglutide, NICE says to consider stopping if less than 5% of starting weight has been lost after 6 months of treatment.
  • A time limit for semaglutide. Under NICE TA875 for weight management, it is used for a maximum of 2 years within a multidisciplinary specialist service. This is not a rule for every semaglutide indication.

One safety point: NICE guidance says tirzepatide should not be used in pregnancy or by women of childbearing potential not using contraception, and semaglutide and liraglutide should not be used in pregnancy. If you are a man whose partner is planning a pregnancy, or you have any other medical question about a medicine, speak to your GP or pharmacist.

A practical way to check your own position

  1. Work out your BMI with the NHS calculator, using your current weight and height.
  2. List any diagnosed conditions from the five above. Only diagnosed conditions count, so if you suspect high blood pressure or sleep apnoea, get it checked first.
  3. Note whether an ethnicity adjustment may apply.
  4. Book a GP appointment and ask where your area is in the rollout and whether you fit a current cohort.
  5. Ask what wrap-around support is offered locally, so you know what to expect.

Bringing a few readings and a short weight history makes the appointment far more productive. For a wider look at how eligibility works across both NHS and private routes, see our guide to weight loss medication eligibility in the UK.

If you don't qualify (yet)

Not meeting the criteria for a particular NHS medicine is not a verdict on whether your health matters. NHS Cornwall and Isles of Scilly ICB notes that other NHS weight services and surgery remain available if you need them, so ask your GP what is offered locally.

While you wait, habits still count. Our comparison of a structured programme vs DIY dieting shows why having a plan and check-ins helps. If you are building movement back in, walking versus structured exercise is a good starting point.

Our perspective

Not meeting a medicine's eligibility criteria should not leave you without useful information or practical support. We think that is the wrong way round. Support should not start only when your health has already taken a hit.

The NHS criteria exist because resources are limited, and they are the right first place to look if you meet them. But qualifying or not, the medicine is only part of it. Food, movement and emotional wellbeing remain useful parts of support; no routine can guarantee a particular treatment result or prevent weight regain.

That is why SHIFT is built around The Programme: Grub for practical food, Fit for achievable movement, Life Back for personal goals and My Timber for check-ins. We support men before, during and after treatment, including those who started elsewhere or want to go without medication. No judgement, plain English, and your privacy respected.

FAQ

Does the NHS cover GLP-1 medicines?

Yes, in England some are available on the NHS, but only for people who meet the national criteria. Tirzepatide may be prescribed in primary care for eligible patients. Under NICE TA875, semaglutide for weight management is provided through specialist services. Separate indications, including cardiovascular-risk reduction, have different guidance. Availability depends on the rollout in your area.

What BMI do you need for Mounjaro on the NHS?

For the national criteria you need a BMI of 35 or more, or 32.5 or more for people from certain ethnic backgrounds. You also need at least four of five listed conditions. From 1 April 2027 a further group with a BMI of 40 or more and three conditions is due to be included.

Can my GP prescribe Wegovy?

For NHS weight management under NICE TA875, semaglutide is provided through a specialist service; ask your GP about the assessment and referral route. A separate NICE recommendation, TA1152, concerns cardiovascular-risk reduction for adults with established cardiovascular disease and a BMI of at least 27. That guidance does not specify a treatment setting. A clinician must decide which indication and pathway apply; neither route guarantees an individual prescription.

What if I don't meet the NHS criteria?

Other NHS weight services and, where needed, surgery remain available, so speak to your GP about what is offered locally. You can also look at structured lifestyle support or private routes, which have their own clinical checks.
