Weight-management review: questions to take with you

A useful conversation starts with the thing you want help with. Choose a topic, pick two or three questions and take them to your GP or treating service.

You can use this whether you are exploring support, receiving treatment elsewhere, or thinking about what happens afterwards.

Need help now? This is a preparation tool, not a symptom checker or a way to contact a clinician. If a concern needs urgent help, use the appropriate healthcare route and the instructions supplied with your medicine. In an emergency, call 999. Do not wait to complete a checklist.

What would you like to discuss?

Choose one group or keep all the questions. You do not need to answer them on this website.

All four question groups are shown below.

Starting the conversation

You do not need to arrive with a diagnosis or a preferred treatment.

  1. What support could fit my health, work and family life?
  2. Which NHS or local services can I access, and how do I get referred?
  3. What other options can we discuss if a medicine is unsuitable or unavailable?

Understanding progress

Bring the pattern you have noticed, including things that have become easier or harder.

  1. Which readings and time period would be useful to compare?
  2. What else should we consider alongside weight, such as everyday function or wellbeing?
  3. What is one realistic change to try, and when should we review it?

Making treatment questions clear

Use the name and formulation on your own medicine label. A checklist cannot decide whether a symptom is safe to wait out.

  1. Could my medicines, symptoms or another health issue need a review?
  2. Which symptoms need urgent help, and which contact route should I use?
  3. What should I do if treatment is interrupted, or I cannot manage eating or drinking?

Leaving with a clear next step

Ask for instructions you can find again, including who is responsible for follow-up.

  1. Who is responsible for my next review, and when should it happen?
  2. If treatment changes or stops, what support will continue and who provides it?
  3. What costs, tests or repeat appointments should I understand, and what should I do if I cannot reach the service?

Before the conversation

A short note on paper or your own phone is enough. If useful, bring:

  • The main thing you want help with and how it affects an ordinary day.
  • The names and formulations of your medicines and supplements, from their labels.
  • Any relevant readings, dates or previous instructions you already have.
  • What you have tried, what helped and what did not fit your week.

You do not need a perfect record. If you cannot remember something, say so.

Before you leave

  • What is the agreed next step?
  • Who is responsible, and when should it happen?
  • Who should you contact if things change or you have not heard back?

This checklist does not recommend a medicine, change a dose or confirm eligibility for treatment.

About this checklist and your information

The question examples are SHIFT editorial prompts, prepared with AI assistance. They have not been independently clinically reviewed and do not provide a diagnosis or individual treatment advice.

This tool has no box for entering health details. Topic choices stay in this page and reset when it reloads; the checklist script does not send or save them. Printing uses your device’s print dialogue. Keep any personal notes somewhere appropriate for you.

Background: NHS information about overweight and obesity, checked 20 October 2026. This supports the general routes to professional help, not endorsement of this checklist.
