Before you use the calculator

What is this calculator for?

Body mass index (BMI) is a simple screening measure that compares your weight with your height. It is used widely in healthcare because it gives a quick, consistent way to estimate whether somebody falls within broad weight categories that may be associated with different levels of health risk. It is useful because it is simple, cheap and easy to repeat — but it is not a diagnosis and it cannot tell the whole story about your health.

What it measures

  • Height
  • Weight
  • A height-to-weight ratio expressed as BMI

Why it is here

BMI can be a useful starting point when thinking about weight-related health risk, deciding whether further assessment may be worthwhile, or tracking broad changes over time.

What this result cannot tell you

No calculator can understand everything about you. Important limitations include:

  • Muscle mass and athletic build
  • Body fat percentage and where fat is carried
  • Ethnicity-related differences in risk thresholds
  • Pregnancy
  • Disability or unusual body composition
  • Your fitness, blood pressure, blood tests, sleep, mood or overall wellbeing
Shift Says

Use BMI as a signpost, not a verdict. If the number is higher than you hoped, it does not mean you have failed — it means you have one more piece of information to work with.

How to use the result sensibly

Use the number as a starting point. Look at the overall trend, how you feel, what is happening with your waist, movement, sleep, blood pressure and other relevant health markers. Where the result is unexpected, concerning or difficult to interpret, a qualified healthcare professional can put it into context.

Industry-standard does not mean individually perfect. These calculators use recognised methods because they are useful, not because they can define a person. Results are informational, subjective in context and open to interpretation.

How this works, limitations & evidence

Interpretation, limitations & evidence

Understand the result — then decide what matters

BMI gives a quick relationship between height and weight. It is widely used by NHS and NICE services, but it is a screening measure, not a diagnosis.

How BMI is calculated

Formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres².

Example: 90 kg at 1.80 m gives 90 ÷ (1.80 × 1.80) = BMI 27.8.

How to interpret an adult BMI

BMIStandard adult classification
Under 18.5Below the standard healthy range
18.5–24.9Healthy weight range
25–29.9Overweight
30+Obesity, with classes increasing as BMI rises

For people from South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean backgrounds, NHS/NICE use lower thresholds for increased cardiometabolic risk: overweight starts at 23 and obesity at 27.5.

Where BMI is useful

Screening

It helps identify people who may benefit from a wider health assessment.

Treatment pathways

BMI is still used in NHS medication and bariatric-surgery criteria, usually alongside health conditions.

Tracking

For one person over time, falling BMI generally reflects falling body weight at the same height.

Where BMI falls short

BMI cannot distinguish fat from muscle, cannot show where fat is stored and does not directly measure fitness, blood pressure, glucose, cholesterol or wellbeing. A muscular person can have a high BMI without high body fat; another person can have a lower BMI but high central adiposity.

Use waist-to-height ratio alongside it

NICE recommends waist-to-height ratio as an additional measure of central adiposity in adults with BMI under 35. A practical public-health message is to keep your waist to less than half your height. Ratios of 0.5–0.59 indicate increased risk and 0.6+ further increased risk.

What should you do with the number?

If BMI is above the healthy reference range, look at the wider picture: waist, blood pressure, sleep, mobility, family history, diabetes risk and how weight is affecting daily life. That is much more useful than treating BMI as a score you have passed or failed.

Common questions

Is BMI accurate?

It is useful at population and screening level but imperfect for individuals.

Can athletes use BMI?

Yes, but high muscle mass can make BMI overestimate body-fat-related risk.

Does BMI decide whether I can have Mounjaro or surgery?

BMI is one part of eligibility; health conditions, local pathways and clinical assessment also matter.
