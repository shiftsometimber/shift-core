Before you use the calculator

What is this calculator for?

A calorie calculator estimates how much energy your body may need each day based on factors such as age, sex, height, weight and activity level. It is designed to give you a practical starting point for understanding energy balance — the relationship between what you eat and drink and what your body uses. The result is an estimate, not a prescription, because real energy needs vary from person to person and from day to day.

What it measures

  • Age
  • Sex
  • Height
  • Weight
  • Activity level
  • Estimated daily energy needs

Why it is here

It can help you understand roughly how much energy you may need to maintain your current weight and, where appropriate, how a modest calorie deficit might support gradual weight loss.

What this result cannot tell you

No calculator can understand everything about you. Important limitations include:

  • Metabolic differences
  • Changes in activity from day to day
  • Medication effects
  • Illness and recovery
  • Muscle mass
  • Accuracy of self-reported activity
  • How easy or hard a target feels in real life
Shift Says

The clever bit is not finding the lowest calorie number you can survive on. It is finding an intake you can actually live with while still eating enough protein, fibre and proper food.

How to use the result sensibly

Use the number as a starting point. Look at the overall trend, how you feel, what is happening with your waist, movement, sleep, blood pressure and other relevant health markers. Where the result is unexpected, concerning or difficult to interpret, a qualified healthcare professional can put it into context.

Industry-standard does not mean individually perfect. These calculators use recognised methods because they are useful, not because they can define a person. Results are informational, subjective in context and open to interpretation.

How this works, limitations & evidence

Interpretation, limitations & evidence

Understand the result — then decide what matters

This calculator estimates the energy needed to maintain your current weight. It uses the Mifflin–St Jeor resting-energy equation and then applies the activity level you select. Your real maintenance intake is the intake that holds average weight broadly stable over time.

How the estimate is calculated

Mifflin–St Jeor equation used for adult men

Resting energy ≈ (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) + 5.

The calculator then multiplies resting energy by the selected activity factor to estimate total daily energy expenditure.

Why your real maintenance may differ

Activity is hard to categorise

“Moderately active” can mean very different things to different people.

Body composition differs

Two men of the same weight and height can have different energy expenditure.

Life changes

Work, illness, sleep, training and weight loss itself can change energy needs.

Turning maintenance into a weight-loss target

NHS Better Health commonly uses a reduction of around 600 kcal/day as a general population starting point for weight loss. That is not compulsory and can be too aggressive or too small for some people.

Do not turn the calculator into a race to the lowest intake.

A useful deficit still needs enough food for protein, fibre, micronutrients and a life you can sustain.

How to calibrate the estimate with real life

Use the estimate for 2–4 weeks. Track average weight under reasonably consistent conditions. If weight is stable, your logged average intake is probably close to maintenance. If weight is trending down or up, adjust gradually rather than making a huge change after three days.

Why plateaus happen

As body weight falls, energy needs usually fall. Activity can also drift down without you noticing, and portion sizes can creep up. A plateau does not prove your metabolism has stopped working.

When calorie counting is not the right tool

If tracking creates obsessive behaviour, significant anxiety or worsens an eating disorder, step away from the calculator and seek individual support. You can create structure through portions, meal templates and food quality without counting every calorie forever.

Common questions

Is this my exact calorie requirement?

No. Predictive equations have individual error and activity multipliers add more uncertainty.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

Usually not automatically; wearable and exercise estimates are rough.

How often should I recalculate?

After a meaningful weight change, major activity change or if your real trend no longer matches the estimate.
